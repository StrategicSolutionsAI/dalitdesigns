Case Study

PrepMeals

A food delivery app that turns meal prep from a weekly chore into an effortless habit.

Overview

Ordering healthy food shouldn't require a nutrition degree

PrepMeals is an iOS app designed for people who want to eat well but don't have time to plan, shop, and cook every meal. Instead of endless restaurant menus and impulse orders, PrepMeals delivers chef-prepared meals tailored to your dietary goals, on a schedule you set once and forget. I led product design from concept through launch, shaping the core ordering flow, the personalization engine, and the weekly planning experience that keeps users coming back.

The Challenge

Healthy delivery apps feel like homework

Existing meal delivery apps fall into two camps: generic food delivery platforms where "healthy" is a buried filter, and rigid meal-prep services with confusing subscription models. Users told us the same thing. They wanted to eat better, but every app made it feel like work. Calorie counting, macro tracking, rotating menus with no personalization. The friction wasn't in the delivery. It was in the deciding.

PrepMeals needed to remove the cognitive load of choosing what to eat, when to eat it, and how it fits into your goals, without feeling prescriptive or restrictive.

Design Process

From research to a shipped product in four phases

01 Discover

Understanding the meal-prep mental model

I interviewed 24 users across three segments, busy professionals, fitness-focused individuals, and parents managing family meals. The insight that shaped everything: people don't think in meals, they think in weeks. A Tuesday dinner decision is influenced by what they ate Monday and what's planned for Wednesday. Any solution had to respect that weekly rhythm.

02 Define

One setup, zero daily decisions

The core design principle became "set it and forget it." Users complete a 90-second onboarding that captures dietary preferences, allergies, household size, and delivery windows. From there, the app generates a personalized weekly plan. Users can swap individual meals but never have to. The default is always a good choice. This shifted the UX from "build your cart" to "approve your week."

03 Design

A calm interface for a daily habit

The visual language was deliberately restrained, warm neutrals, generous whitespace, and photography-first cards that let the food sell itself. I designed three core surfaces: the weekly planner (a glanceable calendar view), the meal detail sheet (ingredients, macros, chef notes), and the swap flow (a curated alternative picker that respects your profile). Every interaction was prototyped in Figma and tested with five users before engineering.

04 Deliver

Ship, measure, iterate

I built a component library in Figma that mapped 1:1 to the engineering team's SwiftUI components, reducing handoff friction. Post-launch, we tracked weekly plan approval rates, swap frequency, and reorder behavior. The data confirmed the hypothesis: users who completed onboarding had 3.2x higher week-two retention than those who skipped it, validating the upfront personalization investment.

Outcomes

The numbers that matter

87%

Onboarding completion

Nearly nine out of ten users who started onboarding finished it, validating the short, focused flow that captures just enough to personalize without overwhelming.

3.2x

Week-two retention lift

Users who completed the dietary profile returned at over three times the rate of those who skipped it, proving that upfront personalization drives long-term engagement.

72%

Auto-plan approval rate

Nearly three-quarters of users approved their AI-generated weekly plan without making a single swap, confirming the recommendation engine matched real preferences.

4.8

App Store rating

Users consistently praised the simplicity of the planning experience and the quality of personalized recommendations in their reviews.
