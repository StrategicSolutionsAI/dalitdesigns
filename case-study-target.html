Case Study

Target

Making dense reporting and operational data easier to scan, compare, and act on through hierarchy and intentional interface decisions.

Role
Senior Product Designer
Timeline
8 months
Platform
Desktop Dashboard
Deliverables
UX/UI, Data Visualization, Design System

The Challenge

Surfacing signal inside layers of operational data

Target's internal reporting tools served dozens of operational teams — from store managers tracking daily metrics to regional leads comparing performance across locations. The dashboards were rich with data but lacked clear visual hierarchy, forcing users to hunt for the numbers that actually mattered to their decisions.

Tables were dense and undifferentiated. Charts used inconsistent scales, colors, and labeling. Users developed workarounds — exporting to spreadsheets, screenshotting charts for comparison — because the interface made it faster to work outside the tool than within it.

The goal was to redesign reporting surfaces so that the most actionable information was immediately scannable, comparisons were intuitive, and the interface could absorb data complexity without passing that complexity along to the user.

Process

Designing clarity into high-density reporting

01
Research & Data Mapping

Understanding how teams actually read dashboards

Observed reporting workflows across multiple team types — store operations, regional management, and merchandising. Mapped which metrics drove decisions versus which were informational noise. Identified the critical first-glance patterns that users relied on to assess whether something needed attention.

02
Information Hierarchy

Establishing a visual priority system

Developed a hierarchy framework that ranked dashboard content by actionability. Primary metrics received visual prominence through size, position, and color. Supporting data was accessible but visually receded. Defined consistent rules for how thresholds, trends, and anomalies surfaced across every view.

03
Interface Design

Building scannable, composable reporting surfaces

Designed a modular card system where each reporting widget followed consistent density and spacing rules. Tables gained visual rhythm through alternating weights and clear row grouping. Charts adopted a unified color language with accessible contrast ratios and consistent axis labeling.

04
Validation & Rollout

Testing with the people who live in these screens

Ran usability sessions with store managers and regional leads using real data sets. Measured time-to-insight on key reporting tasks and iterated on layout density, filter placement, and comparison workflows. Rolled out changes incrementally to allow teams to adapt without disrupting existing routines.

Solution

A dashboard built for faster decisions
